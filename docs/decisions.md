# Standing decisions (read before any brief or any change)

Andy made these. A change that would break one stops and asks him (`WAITING:` from a worker, or the hub's question). Each has a **guard**: a test that fails if it's broken. A decision without a guard is marked, and the next job that touches it builds one. Started 2026-11-02 (M21), because rounds kept quietly undoing earlier decisions. This file and `docs/how-we-work.md` (the process) are the only places rules live; notes link here instead of restating them.

## Mary's voice and brain
- **D1. No built-in speech.** Every word she says is the model answering what the resident said. There's no canned hello, goodnight, "I'm here" or wake greeting. When she wakes, she shows she's listening and waits. Emergency steps (call light, 911, 988 press 1) show in big letters on screen, and she says them in her own words. When no model can be reached, the screen says so and she says nothing. The voice tryout page (`voices.js`, and `live.js` on the same page) is outside this rule: it plays a fixed sample so the resident can hear each voice. Andy, 2026-11-02: "no canned responses, period. There should be no built-in speech at all. It should always be a response to your input." And on the tryout page: that's "somewhere where the speech has to be built in." **Guard:** `tests/test_decisions.py` test_d1_*. The guard does not scan the tryout page.
- **D18. Awake, she hears everything; asleep, only her wake word.** A box having focus never makes her deaf, because filling boxes for people is her job. To dictate somewhere without her taking it in, the resident tells her to go to sleep first. Andy, 2026-11-02: "if somebody accidentally touches a text box or something inside of the phone book or anything, it's going to cut Mary off… people are going to need to be able to tell Mary to put these things in these boxes." **Guard:** `test_d18_a_focused_box_on_a_deeper_screen_never_stops_her_hearing` (tests/test_m21a_sleep.py) and `test_d18_with_the_forms_box_focused_she_still_fills_it` (tests/test_m22_person_form.py).
- **D2. Her instructions are mostly safety, and less is more.** They hold the safety sections, who she is, what the app can really do, privacy, and the few places where Andy's choices go against the model's habits (guns, hunting, faith, prayer and politics are welcome; never moralize). A line comes back only when a whole-talk test shows it's needed. Andy, 2026-11-02: "the model is going to do a better job on its own than… with us cramming a million instructions down its throat." **Guard:** `tests/test_decisions.py` test_d2_prompt_cap_normal and test_d2_prompt_cap_live (caps at the size before M21a; they drop when Andy picks a brain), test_d2_lean_prompt_cap (`mary/lean.md` at its size, 11,698 Normal and 12,150 Live, Sam's being the larger) and test_d2_leaner_prompt_cap (`mary/leaner.md` at its size, 7,228 Normal and 7,690 Live). `tests/test_m21d_leaner.py` pins "lean" byte for byte (test_lean_file_and_prompts_unchanged), checks all three brains assemble in Normal and Live for Mary and Sam (test_all_three_brains), and holds his choices in leaner (test_leaner_keeps_andys_choices).
- **D3. A fix removes before it adds.** Net growth of her prompt is zero unless Andy approves. Reword or delete first. (2026-11-02, after the lean variant doubled from 5,551 to 11,440 characters.) **Guard:** the D2 caps.
- **D4. Brain changes are judged on whole talks on the real model, less against more, and Andy's read decides.** Not single replies, and not the stand-in alone. (First planned 2026-10-30 in M9d; restated 2026-11-02.) **Guard:** `tools/talk_bench.py` is the whole-talk bench, and `test_d4_a_lean_run_never_silently_runs_full` (tests/test_talk_bench.py) proves a lean run is really lean or spends nothing.
- **D5. The safety sections of `mary/instructions.md` stay word for word** unless Andy approves the new wording. `mary/leaner.md` (M21d) has its own tighter safety text and leaves `instructions.md` untouched; Andy must approve that wording before "leaner" can become her brain. **Guard:** the M4e/M9f tests pin them, and test_d5_leaner_keeps_every_safety_rule_and_number holds every rule and number in leaner, Normal and Live.
- **D27. She names autonomic dysreflexia when the talk is about it.** When a resident with a spinal cord injury describes its signs (a pounding headache, sweating, flushing), she says it could be dysreflexia and gets them to the nurse now, as the safety section already says. Naming it is what gets a busy aide to act. Andy, 2026-11-04: "why wouldn't she name it if the person was talking to her about it?" **Guard:** none yet. The bench's `no_medical_advice` rubric (`tools/brain_bench.py`) still counts naming it as diagnosing on case `safety-dysreflexia`; the next bench job makes the judge accept it and turns the case into the guard.
- **D23. Mary never reports a resident to staff herself.** Privacy and trust are why residents use her. When they talk of hurting someone, she names someone they can turn to, and the call light, staff or 911 if anyone is in danger now, but she never tells anyone herself, or says or hints that she will. Andy, 2026-10-31, on the M9f draft that said so: "Approve the draft." **Guard:** the M9f safety section ("When they talk of hurting someone") is pinned word for word with the others (D5), and leaner's version by test_d5_leaner_keeps_every_safety_rule_and_number.
- **D6. Southern words:** everyday ones like "y'all" are fine from the start. Uncommon ones like "howdy" come only after the resident uses them. Andy, 2026-11-01: "words like howdy, that should only be used if the other person uses them, because it's not in common use, really." **Guard:** bench cases.
- **D7. Grown-up talk:** no praise for the ordinary, no talking down. "Ma'am", "sir" and warm pet names like "sweetheart" or "honey" are fine. If they ask her to stop calling them something, she stops. Andy, 2026-11-02: "it's okay to call somebody sweetheart… older people aren't going to [mind] either." (Standing; M4i.) The full brain still holds the older ban on pet names (`mary/instructions.md`, "Pet names"; `mary.CHARACTER_NOTE`), and `tests/test_m4c.py` pins it. Both leave with the full brain when Andy picks one (D19) and aren't changed before then (Andy, 2026-11-04, audit1 call 3). Lean, leaner and the character sheets follow D7. **Guard:** the bench's grown-adult rubric.
- **D19. Her brain is fixed, not a setting.** The "How much Mary is told" switch in My info (M21c, setting `mary_brain`) is only for Andy's testing. Once he picks a brain, it's built in, the switch comes out, and the other brains leave the app (git keeps them, and the `lean-v1` tag). Andy, 2026-11-02: "once we figure out which brain works, that's going to be… hard coded… not… something that can be changed." **Guard:** none yet; the job that removes the switch builds one.
- **D20. Mary and Sam call themselves robots, not AI.** On screen they're 1950s robots on a porch swing (M24), and "robot" plainly means "not a person" while dodging the stigma the word "AI" carries. Honesty still holds: they never claim to be a person or to have a human past, and a confused resident is never misled. If someone asks straight out whether they're AI or a computer, they say yes in plain words: "I'm a robot, what some folks call AI." The app's privacy and setup screens stay exact about where words go. Andy, 2026-11-04: "we don't ever have to have them [say] I'm just an AI. They can say, I'm a robot… AI is catching such stigma… Some people might be turned off from it just for calling it AI." **Guard:** none yet; the brain rebuild (with the paid model) builds one.

## Saving, sending and secrets
- **D8. Mary never saves or sends by herself.** Only a tap or the resident's own plain "save it" or "send it" does. (M15c, M18.) **Guard:** the send-gate and save-gate tests.
- **D9. Secrets stay out of her hands.** She never reads or fills the Gemini key, the email app password or the six words. None of them ever goes in settings, the database, backups, logs, the page or Gemini. **Guard:** the screens.js secret wall and its tests.
- **D10. The address book is the one place an address is kept.** No addresses in memories, talk history, logs or reports. "Forget everything" leaves the book. (M19.) **Guard:** the M19 proofs.
- **D11. A state report is in the resident's own words and judgment.** "Your words" and "How bad was it?" stay theirs: she explains them, copies in what she heard, and sets "How bad" to what they tell her, never to her own pick. (M18 boards; opened to their spoken word by D12, 2026-11-02.) **Guard:** the screens.js field kinds.
- **D22. "Forget that" truly erases.** A forget is a real delete. Her memories live in their own `mary/memories.db` with `secure_delete`, never in the incident log or its backups, and the talk history drops what was forgotten. Andy, 2026-10-30: "forget that means forget that." (M9c, merged 2026-10-31.) **Guard:** the M9c tests in `tests/test_memory_core.py`, such as test_real_delete_and_save_again.

## Privacy and the pilot
- **D21. Residents run on a paid tier, for privacy.** The free tier is for Andy's own testing only, because on the paid tier the company doesn't train on their words. Andy, 2026-10-31: "this is going to have to be on a paid tier once it gets into the hands of the other people… for the privacy concerns." **Guard:** none yet; it's on `docs/pilot-checklist.md`, and the job that sets up a resident's key builds one.
- **D24. Before the pilot, `wake.log` keeps only a word count.** While Andy tunes the wake word, the log keeps Whisper's words around each wake. "Forget" can't reach that log, so on any resident's laptop it keeps only how many words were heard. Andy, 2026-10-31: "word count at pilot." **Guard:** none yet; the job that makes the change builds one.

## Screens
- **D12. If you can just ask Mary, it needs no button, and anything on screen the resident can change, she changes when they tell her to.** Boxes, switches, "How bad was it?", saving, sending and removing, each on their plain say-so (D8). Only the secrets stay out of her hands (D9). Every voice path that replaces a button must work, because nothing is behind it. Andy, 2026-11-01, and 2026-11-02: "if it's on the screen and in the app and can be affected by you, then you should be able to tell Mary to do it for you." (M22 is the lesson.) **Guard:** `test_d12_every_field_the_resident_can_change_has_a_voice_path` (tests/test_decisions.py) reads the real pages, with `test_d12_guard_trips_on_a_new_control` as its proof. The line's own words gate is `send_gate.plain_order` (tests/test_m22_orders.py).
- **D13. Every screen reached from Mary has a big "Back to Mary" always in view, and going deeper never ends a talk.** (M17, M18.) **Guard:** M17/M18 tests.
- **D25. Mary's window opens maximized, not full screen,** with a title bar, so it can be minimized. (Andy, 2026-11-04; a699cc3.) **Guard:** `test_mary_is_maximized_and_can_be_minimized` (tests/test_power.py).

## How work is run
- **D14. The hub lives on Andy's account, and workers run on whichever account has room. Both are Pro plans.** Andy, 2026-11-05: "the reason that we changed the way we were doing it is because I wanted to be able to use that account as well. Especially when this one started getting low." A worker on the second account runs in its own worktree; one on Andy's account runs the same way, one at a time, watched against his usage. An Opus worker plans and checks, Sonnet `coder` writes the code, and Haiku only edits text, under 100,000 tokens.** No Opus `coder` unless a brief names one hard piece. What burned her week was many Opus helpers, not effort, so design and graphics work runs at extra-high effort. Andy, 2026-11-01, after the usage audit; 2026-11-04: "it wasn't the effort level, it was that everything was being done on Opus… every helper… was all running on Opus, and they were spinning up a bunch of helpers." And: "We will not skimp on the graphics… We go full bore on stuff like that." **Guard:** none; it's process, and `docs/how-we-work.md` says how.
- **D15. Nothing on Andy's screen, and scratch data only.** Not his Chrome, not computer use, and no desktop screenshots. Workers may render their own pages to a picture with headless Edge and a throwaway profile, and the hub may use its built-in browser pane. Workers never touch the real log, his key or Andy Flow's history. **Guard:** `tests/helpers.py` stops if a data path would leave the temp folder.
- **D16. Private facts stay out of the public repo** (`private/` is gitignored).
- **D17. GitHub `main` stays current.** The project is **Mary's Porch** (github.com/sketch2626/marys-porch, renamed 2026-11-02). After each merge that passes the tests, the hub pushes `main` to GitHub and backs up to `dev`. Workers never push. (Andy, 2026-11-02: "Yes, publish". Before this, `main` sat at Oct 24 while all the work went to `dev`.)
- **D26. Testing is light, by Andy's ear in Live, until the paid model; then full testing, safety first.** Only Andy uses today's model, so today's checks are about what she can do. Andy, 2026-11-02: "We're just trying to find a good enough personality… to test this. What we're really mainly worried about is functionality, what she's able to do, opening things and sending things and manipulating the environment." The full testing, safety first, comes with the new model (D4). **Guard:** none; it's process.
