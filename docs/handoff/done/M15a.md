**Why:** this is the first piece of cards and messages (M15), which Andy approved with all 12 recommendations. Residents forget family birthdays and feel bad about it. Mary should remember the dates they gave her and raise each one once, a week ahead, so they can call, write or send a card.

**Build:**
1. **Save dates.** When a resident says "Mark's birthday is November 7th" (or "my anniversary is June 3rd", or "my granddaughter Lily turns ten on the 12th of March"), keep the memory as today, and also save the date as month and day, who it's for, and what it is. Dates come only from what the resident said. Never guess one, and never take one from anywhere else.
   - Prefer a plain, offline parser in Python that works with no Gemini. Spoken forms matter: "November seventh", "the 7th of November", "11/7". A date it can't read is not saved as a date; the plain memory still is.
   - If she corrects it ("no, it's the 9th"), the new date replaces the old one.
2. **The reminder.** It comes in the first talk that falls 8 to 5 days before the date, so a missed day doesn't lose it.
   - It reaches her brain as one quiet turn note, and she says it in her own words. One mind, one voice: no pop-up and no fixed line.
   - Then it's marked done for that year, whatever they answer. It never comes up twice, across restarts too.
   - It works in Normal and in Live.
3. **Never for someone who has died.** If a memory says that person has passed ("my late husband", "Mark passed in 2019", "since Ruth died"), there's no reminder. "Don't remind me about him" (or her, or a name) stops that person's reminders for good.
4. **Forget really forgets.** "Forget Mark's birthday", `forget_name` and "forget everything" also remove the dates and their done-marks.
5. **No card offer yet.** Cards come in M15b. The note may say she can help them call or write, but it offers a card only if a `messages` skill is installed. Leave a clear hook for M15b.
6. **What Mary remembers** in My info shows a saved date in plain words: "Mark's birthday, November 7".
