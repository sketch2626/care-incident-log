Notes for the builder (M4)

Andy picked caption style A (Coloring-in), ear waves for listening and a thought cloud for thinking (2026-10-30). Every class is in mockups/mary/round6/r6.css; full notes in round6/README.md.

1. Word timing: estimated

Gemini TTS, Live and Kokoro give no per-word times (M2, 2026-10-29: Live sent no startOffset/endOffset). So estimate, the same way the live board does:

· Weight of a word = its syllables (vowel groups) + 0.5. Pauses: comma 1.5, end of sentence 3.

· Start = chunk start + chunk audio length × (weight before it ÷ total weight). It fills over its own share, kept between 180 and 900 ms.

· Run it on the playback clock (the AudioContext time of the scheduled audio), not the wall clock, so words wait if the sound stalls.

2. Each talk mode

· Live: her words arrive as text pieces (outputTranscription) next to the audio. Spread each piece's words over the audio that came with it, and re-anchor at every piece so errors never add up. If Live ever sends startOffset/endOffset (the log already checks word_timings), use those instead.

· Normal (Gemini voice) and Offline (Kokoro): make speech one sentence at a time; plan each sentence's words once its audio length is known. While still streaming, start at about 2.6 words a second and correct.

3. Showing the words

· Lay out the whole two-line chunk first, later words hidden but holding their place, so nothing jumps. Break lines by measured width, never inside a word.

· The next word appears as an empty outline 150 ms early, then fills left to right. The word being said lifts 5 px.

· Two lines at most. When a third starts, the top line floats up 40 px and fades over 400 ms while the rest slide up.

· Fade out 4 s after she stops, or as soon as the resident talks. If she is cut off (Live), stop filling, keep the said words, fade the rest in 300 ms.

4. Emergency

Show the whole sentence at once, already filled; the word being said still lifts. Steady glow, never flashing. The two answer buttons sit above the words: the only time more than one button shows.

5. Sizes and fonts

· Captions 66 px on a 1280 × 800 screen; scale with the window, about clamp(48px, 5.2vw, 88px).

· Menu button 96 px tall, menu rows 88 px, menu text 32 px. Nothing under 64 px to tap or 20 px to read.

· Fredoka 600 is free (SIL Open Font License). Bundle it as woff2 in web/mary/fonts/; Mary must work with no internet.

6. Listening and thinking

· Listening: three arcs on each side where her ears would be, pulsing outward small to large. No words, no bar. They and the blue glow brighten with the mic level (Live: a Web Audio analyser on the mic; Normal: GET /api/listen/level).

· Thinking runs from the end of the resident's turn to her first sound, shown only after 400 ms so quick replies don't flicker. Bubbles rise off her head into a cloud up and to her left; her face eases 60 px lower so it fits, and back up when she talks.

· Build one face with mood classes on the screen: .listen, .listen.loud, .think.dots or .think.word. The live board works this way.

7. The one button

· Top right, always there. A tap opens the menu; "Hide menu", a tap on her face, or 20 s with no tap closes it. She scoots aside while it is open.

· Labels follow the companion's name. Talk stays first until wake words (M6) are reliable; Games says "Coming soon" until M7.

8. Accessibility

· The caption itself is hidden from screen readers (her voice already speaks); each finished sentence goes to a polite live region once.

· Reduced motion: no sway, float or lift, but the words still fill.

· Color is never the only signal: words also lift or underline, and every glow comes with a face change.
