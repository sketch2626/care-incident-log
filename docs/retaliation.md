# If You Are Punished for Complaining (Retaliation)

You have the right to complain about your care. The nursing home is not allowed to punish you for it. This page explains what retaliation is, what the law says, and what you can do.

This page gives general information. It is not legal advice.

## What is retaliation?

Retaliation (also called "reprisal") means someone treats you worse because you complained or used your rights.

The federal rule says a nursing home must let you use your rights "without interference, coercion, discrimination, or reprisal." The federal government (CMS) has named two examples in its guidance for inspectors:

- Keeping a resident out of group activities as punishment.
- Staff retaliating against a resident. CMS says retaliation by staff is abuse.

Other things can also be retaliation if they happen because you complained. Here are some examples:

- Your care gets slower. Your call light is ignored. Staff come late or not at all.
- Someone threatens you. For example: "We will send you somewhere else," or "We will call your family."
- Someone threatens to discharge you, or tells you that you have to leave.
- You are moved to a different room or hall as a punishment.
- Your family or friends are told to stay away, or your visits are cut short.
- A staff member is rough with you, yells at you, or tries to scare you.
- Someone tells you to stop complaining, or tells you not to talk to anyone outside.
- Something you need or enjoy is taken away.

If the timing fits ("it started right after I complained"), write that down. It matters.

## Your protections

**Federal law (applies in every nursing home that takes Medicare or Medicaid)**

- **Complaining is your right.** You may complain about your care, the staff, other residents, or anything else about your stay, to the nursing home or to an outside agency, with no punishment and no fear of punishment. (42 CFR 483.10(j)(1), tag F585)
- **You may complain without giving your name.** The home must tell you that you can complain out loud or in writing, and must give you the contact information for the State agency and the State Long-Term Care Ombudsman. (42 CFR 483.10(j)(4)(i), tag F585)
- **You may use all of your rights freely.** The home may not interfere with you, force you, treat you differently, or get back at you for using your rights. (42 CFR 483.10(b)(1) and (b)(2), tag F550)
- **You may stay in touch with the outside.** You have the right to private phone calls, to keep and use your own cell phone at your own expense, to reasonable use of the internet if the home has it, and to send and get mail. (42 CFR 483.10(g)(6)-(9), tag F576)
- **Staff retaliation is abuse.** CMS tells inspectors that retaliation by staff is abuse, even if the staff member says they did not mean to hurt you. (42 CFR 483.12, CMS guidance for tag F600)
- **Complaining is not a reason to discharge you.** A nursing home can only move or discharge you for six reasons: your needs cannot be met there, your health got better, your presence puts other people's safety at risk, your presence puts other people's health at risk, you did not pay, or the home is closing. Complaining is not on the list. (42 CFR 483.15(c)(1)(i), tag F627)
- **You can appeal a discharge.** If you appeal, the home generally cannot make you leave until the appeal is decided. The only exception is when waiting would put you or others in danger, and the home has to write down why. (42 CFR 483.15(c)(1)(ii), tag F627)
- **You must get written notice first.** The notice must say why, give the date, explain how to appeal, and list the phone number and address of the State Long-Term Care Ombudsman. It must usually come at least 30 days before. (42 CFR 483.15(c)(3)-(5), tag F628)

**North Carolina law**

- **The Patients' Bill of Rights.** A North Carolina nursing home patient may bring up problems and ask for changes, for themselves or for other people, to staff, the administrator, the community advisory committee, the state Department, or other people or groups, "without fear of reprisal, restraint, interference, coercion, or discrimination." (NCGS 131E-117(12))
- **Limits on discharge.** A patient may not be transferred or discharged except for medical reasons, the patient's own welfare or the welfare of other patients, nonpayment, or when Medicare or Medicaid rules require it. (NCGS 131E-117(15))
- **No giving up your rights.** A nursing home cannot make you sign away these rights. (NCGS 131E-119)
- **Your name is kept private.** The state Department of Health and Human Services must keep secret the name of anyone who files a complaint with it. (NCGS 131E-124(c)) If a family member files for you, they can only get news of the case if you, your legal representative or your responsible party gives written permission. (NCGS 131E-124(c))

## What to do

1. **Write it down.** Use a notebook, or use this app. Write the date and time. Write what happened, who did it or said it, and who saw it. Write what you complained about and when. Keep any papers you are given.
2. **Call it retaliation.** Say it out loud or write it: "I think this is retaliation for my complaint." Mark it that way in this app, too.
3. **Call the Ombudsman.** The North Carolina Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program helps residents for free. State office: **(919) 855-3400.** There is also a regional ombudsman for each county. The state web page has a list by county: https://www.ncdhhs.gov/divisions/aging/long-term-care-ombudsman
4. **Report it to the state.** The Division of Health Service Regulation (DHSR) Complaint Intake Unit: **1-800-624-3004** (toll-free in North Carolina) or 919-855-4500. Phone hours are weekdays, 9 a.m. to 12 noon and 1 p.m. to 4 p.m., except holidays. You can also mail or fax a complaint. Mail: Complaint Intake Unit, 2711 Mail Service Center, Raleigh, NC 27699-2711. Fax: 919-715-7724. The state can only look into things that happened in the past year, so do not wait. Say that you are being punished for complaining.
5. **Think abuse or a crime?** If someone is hurt or in danger right now, call **911**. To report abuse, neglect, or theft from an older or disabled adult, call the Department of Social Services (Adult Protective Services) in your county. Find your county here: https://www.ncdhhs.gov/localDSS
6. **Living with a disability?** Disability Rights North Carolina is the state's official protection and advocacy group: **1-877-235-4210** (toll-free in North Carolina).
7. **If you get a discharge or transfer notice,** do not sign anything you do not understand. Call the Ombudsman right away and ask about an appeal. The notice must list how to appeal.

You can ask a family member, friend, or the Ombudsman to make the call with you or for you.

## Words the app can watch for

If a resident says something like this, the app can ask: "Do you think this is happening because you complained?"

- "because I complained"
- "after I complained"
- "since I reported it"
- "ever since I said something"
- "threatened to discharge me"
- "said I have to leave"
- "they want to send me somewhere else"
- "they are punishing me"
- "they ignore my call light now"
- "nobody comes when I ring"
- "they moved me to another room"
- "they won't let me go to activities"
- "they won't let my family visit"
- "told my visitors to leave"
- "she was rough with me"
- "they yelled at me for complaining"
- "told me to stop complaining"
- "told me not to tell anyone"
- "they call me a troublemaker"
- "I'm afraid to say anything"
- "I'm scared of what they will do"
- "they are getting back at me"
