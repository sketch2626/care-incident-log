4. **How the cards look (the most important part).**
   - **Grown up and traditional:**
     - heavy cream or white paper;
     - deep, muted colors (navy, forest green, burgundy, gold);
     - classic serif or engraved-script lettering, perhaps with a gold-foil look.
     - Think of the best rack at a drugstore: a card for a man in his fifties, for a mother, for a grandson.
   - **None of these:** cartoon balloons, confetti, clip art, flat primary colors, or bubbly "fun" fonts.
   - **Pictures that look painted:** scenes or botanicals built in SVG, with gradients, soft edges and a paper-grain texture (SVG filters). Or silhouettes against a painted sky: a lake at dawn with one fishing boat, a lighthouse, a magnolia branch, a buck at the edge of a field. Fit each one to what Mary knows about the person.
   - **Fonts:**
     - Load them from Google Fonts in the boards (the canvas allows that). Don't download any files.
     - Choose fonts with an open license that the app can bundle later, and name them in the README: one handwriting font that reads like real cursive, one plain handwriting font, and one classic font for the verse.
   - **Sizes:** screen text stays at least 20px. On the card itself, the verse and their words must read easily at the card's on-screen size.
